Four Principles of Reliable System Architecture

Designing systems that handle failures, scale efficiently, respond faster, and prevent duplicate operations.

Learn how reliable system design works through practical examples from a food delivery application.

Explore the Principles

What happens when a customer places an order?

Five steps run in a few seconds. Each one can go wrong.

  1. Step 1Customer places an order
  2. Step 2The application validates the order
  3. Step 3The payment service processes the payment
  4. Step 4The order is stored in the database
  5. Step 5The delivery partner receives the notification
Payment service becomes unavailable.
Checkout becomes slow because of extra tasks.
The customer retries a request and creates a risk of duplicate orders.

Building Systems That Can Be Trusted

Design for Failure
Handle failures safely.
Stateful vs. Stateless Architecture
Manage application state carefully and support scaling.
Decoupling Hot Paths from Heavy Writes
Move suitable secondary tasks to background processing.
Idempotency
Prevent duplicate effects during retries.

We cannot prevent every failure, but we can design our systems to handle failures safely without unnecessarily affecting customers or important business operations.
